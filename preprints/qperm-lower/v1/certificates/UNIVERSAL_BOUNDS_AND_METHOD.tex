Let $H:M_n(\mathbb C)\to M_n(\mathbb C)$ be complex linear, ordinary positive,
unital and trace preserving. Define
\[
 \Phi_n(H)=\frac1{n!}\det(\partial_X)\det H(X).
\]
The entries of $X$ are independent complex variables. Every bound in this
note is uniform over all such maps; no additional parameter of $H$ is fixed.

\section{The main bound and the slightly tighter refinements}
\begin{theorem}[One formula throughout $n\ge8$]
For every positive bistochastic map $H$,
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_n(H)\ge-\frac{n}{n^2+6n+39}\qquad(n\ge8).}
\]
For $n\ge17$, the following slightly tighter bound is available:
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_n(H)\ge-\frac{n-7}{n^2-n+6}.}
\]
For $n\ge17$, one can further replace the constant $6$ by
\[
 K_{17}=\frac{32068818512}{5176217579}=6.195415479\ldots
\]
in the expression
\[
 h_n(K)=-\frac{n-1-K}{n(n-1)+K}.
\]
The additional certified rank-eighteen calculation in this note gives
$K_{18}=32532964294/5171460901=6.290865369\ldots$ for $n\ge18$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exact finite dual certificates in dimensions $8,\ldots,16$ dominate
$-n/(n^2+6n+39)$. Their values are listed below, and the comparison uses
rational arithmetic. The rank-sixteen certificate is at least $-3/82$.
Its exact dimension-transport certificate gives $h_n(6)$ for every $n\ge17$.
The all-dimensional comparison is
\[
 h_n(6)+\frac{n}{n^2+6n+39}
 =\frac{9n+273}{(n^2-n+6)(n^2+6n+39)}>0.
\]
The rank-seventeen seed and its transport certificate give the final
refinement. The identity $h_k(K(k,\gamma_k))=\gamma_k$ supplies the seed
dimension itself: transport from $k+1$ therefore gives a combined range
$n\ge k$, with no gap. The same observation applies to the new rank-eighteen
refinement. The finite and transport certificates are included in the package.
\end{proof}

\section{The theorem that turns finite improvements into universal ones}
\begin{theorem}[Dimension transport]
Suppose $k\ge3$ and $\gamma\in[-1/k,0]$ satisfy
$\Phi_k(G)\ge\gamma$ for every positive bistochastic $G:M_k\to M_k$.
Put
\[
 K(k,\gamma)=\frac{(k-1)(1+k\gamma)}{1-\gamma}.
\]
Then every positive bistochastic map in every dimension $n\ge k+3$ satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_n(H)\ge h_n(K(k,\gamma)).}
\]
A finite exact coefficient test can reduce the threshold to $k+1$.
That stronger threshold has been verified for the seeds used in the displayed
large-dimension refinements.
\end{theorem}
When the threshold $k+1$ is certified, the seed endpoint
$h_k(K(k,\gamma))=\gamma$ extends the resulting bound to every $n\ge k$.

Here $K$ is a number extracted from a universal certificate. It is not an
assumption on the particular map $H$. The theorem therefore produces
dimension-only bounds.

The gain from improving a seed is explicit. For $\gamma'>\gamma$,
\[
 K(k,\gamma')-K(k,\gamma)
 =\frac{(k^2-1)(\gamma'-\gamma)}{(1-\gamma')(1-\gamma)}>0.
\]
For $K'>K$, with $N=n(n-1)$,
\[
 \boxed{h_n(K')-h_n(K)
 =\frac{(K'-K)(n^2-1)}{(N+K')(N+K)}>0.}
\]
Thus one strict seed improvement, provided it raises $K$ beyond the existing
maximum, improves the entire tail of the universal bound. The exact gains
are checked by \texttt{compile\_bounds.py}.

\subsection{A first additional iteration improves the entire $n\ge8$ statement}
The new calculation proves, by a rational feasible dual,
\[
 \gamma_{18}=-\frac{171460901}{5000000000}=-0.0342921802.
\]
Its transport constant is strictly greater than $31/5$, and the exact
polynomial residual certificate verifies transport from $n=19$. The seed
itself supplies $n=18$. Combining this with the finite certificates in
dimensions $8,\ldots,17$ gives
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_n(H)\ge-\frac{5n}{5n^2+31n+190}\qquad(n\ge8).}
\]
Indeed, all those finite comparisons hold over $\Q$, and the tail follows
from $h_n(31/5)$: after the two denominators are multiplied out, its gap above
$-n/(n^2+(31/5)n+38)$ has numerator
$\frac{321}{25}n+\frac{1368}{5}>0$.
This new simple formula improves the original one in every dimension of the
same range, since
\[
 -\frac{5n}{5n^2+31n+190}+\frac{n}{n^2+6n+39}
 =\frac{n(n-5)}{(5n^2+31n+190)(n^2+6n+39)}>0\quad(n\ge8).
\]
Its leading order remains $-1/n$; the improvement is in the certified
correction terms. The cleaner $h_n(6)$ and the stronger subsequent
transport constants remain useful large-dimension refinements.

\section{Why the transport theorem is a positivity argument}
Write the orbital determinant polynomial as
\[
 f_H(x)=\mathbb E_U\det H(U\operatorname{diag}(x)U^*)
 =\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}w_\lambda
 \frac{m_\lambda(x)}{m_\lambda(1^n)}.
\]
Positivity and unitality imply $w_\lambda\ge0$ and $\sum w_\lambda=1$.
The target is the exact signed linear functional
\[
 \Phi_n(H)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}
 \frac{(-1)^{n-\ell(\lambda)}}{\binom n{\ell(\lambda)}}w_\lambda.
\]
For $k$ active variables, define
\[
 \Delta_k=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j),\quad
 \D_k=\Delta_k^{-1}\partial_1\cdots\partial_k\Delta_k,
 \quad\T_k=(\partial_1+\cdots+\partial_k)^k.
\]
The crucial inherited inequality is
\[
 (\D_k-\gamma_k\T_k)f_H\ge_{\rm coeff}0.
\]
It comes from compatible positive maps obtained by compressing the same $H$.
Expanding positive baseline matrices into rank-one pieces gives nonnegative
Gram weights multiplying lower-dimensional determinant contractions. A
capacity-normalization argument extends the bistochastic seed to those
unnormalized compressed maps. Positivity is used before averaging; no closure
of orbital averages under stability or hyperbolicity is assumed.

The Schur identity
\[
 \D_ks_\lambda=
 \prod_{i=1}^k(\lambda_i+k-i)s_{\lambda-(1^k)}
\]
gives an exact coefficient calculus. The value is zero if $\lambda_k=0$.
This identity makes the finite models computable using integer/rational
linear algebra.

One selected coefficient row is
\[
 J=\sum_{\mu\vdash k}\frac{(k)_s}{(n)_s}
 \left(\frac{(-1)^j}{\binom kj}-\gamma\right)
 w_{\mu\cup1^{n-k}}\ge0,
 \quad j=k-\ell(\mu),\quad s=k-m_1(\mu).
\]
Together with
\[
 R=\frac{w_{(n-1,1)}}{n-1}-w_{(n)}\ge0,
\]
this yields a certificate of the form
\[
 \Phi_n-h_n(K)=tJ+\mathbf{1}_{n\text{ even}}(1/n+h_n(K))R
 +\sum_\lambda c_\lambda w_\lambda,
 \qquad t\ge0,\quad c_\lambda\ge0.
\]
After denominators are cleared, the finitely many selected residuals become
polynomials in $n$. Nonnegative rational coefficients after the shift
$n=n_0+z$ certify the entire range $n\ge n_0$, not a finite sample of
dimensions. A separate analytic argument proves the general threshold $k+3$.
The complete proof and parity correction are in
\texttt{transport/transport\_general.md}.

\section{How to obtain and validate the stronger seeds}
At a fixed dimension retain nonnegative orbital weights, their normalization,
the square-free coefficient floor, the complete characteristic-coefficient
hulls of projection images, and every inherited mixed coefficient inequality.
This gives a rational linear program
\[
 \min c^Tx\quad\text{subject to }Ax=b,\quad x\ge0.
\]
Every actual map gives a feasible point. A dual vector satisfying
\[
 A^Ty\le c
\]
therefore proves the universal floor $\gamma=b^Ty$.

Numerical optimization proposes $y$. The implementation converts it to
rational entries, repairs feasibility using the normalization blocks, and
recomputes every residual $c-A^Ty$ exactly. A candidate that fails exact
feasibility or fails to improve the retained bound is not counted as progress.
The feasible dual, not the optimizer's printed objective, is the certificate.

The scripts included here separate the stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{finite/checks/sparse\_extend.py}: discover and rationally certify
one or more new finite-dimensional floors.
\item \texttt{finite/checks/verify\_all.py}: reconstruct the models and verify
the archived duals without trusting numerical optimization.
\item \texttt{compile\_bounds.py}: transform the certified seeds into exact
all-dimensional transport certificates, compare the bounds, and produce a
single-formula envelope valid from dimension eight.
\end{enumerate}
Run commands from the method directory. For example, a next-rank discovery attempt after
the rank-eighteen archive is
\begin{verbatim}
python3 finite/checks/sparse_extend.py 19 19 6
python3 finite/checks/verify_all.py
python3 compile_bounds.py
\end{verbatim}
The discovery attempt does not guarantee a strict improvement. Numerical
discovery needs SciPy; the certificate verification and transport
use exact rational arithmetic. The rank and spectral-degree arguments are
explicit so a failed numerical proposal can be diagnosed without weakening
the acceptance test.

\section{Keeping the validity range fixed as certificates improve}
Let $\mathcal C_t$ be the certificates retained after stage $t$, and let $n_i$
be the proved transport threshold for certificate $i$. Define, for every
$n\ge8$,
\[
 B_t(n)=\max\left\{-\frac{n}{n^2+6n+39},\quad
 \gamma_n\ \text{if certified},\quad
 h_n(K_i):i\in\mathcal C_t,\ n\ge n_i\right\}.
\]
Then $\Phi_n(H)\ge B_t(n)$ for all $n\ge8$, and retaining old certificates
ensures $B_{t+1}(n)\ge B_t(n)$ pointwise. Improvements never require withdrawing
the original range. Strict improvement at every stage is not guaranteed.

A useful rational-envelope certificate avoids a long piecewise statement.
Suppose $h_n(K)$ is known for $n\ge n_0$, and choose constants $A,B$ with
$n^2+An+B>0$ on $n\ge8$. The comparison
\[
 h_n(K)\ge-\frac{n}{n^2+An+B}
\]
is equivalent, on the tail, to nonnegativity of
\[
 (K-A)n^2+[K+A(K+1)-B]n+B(K+1).
\]
Together with the finitely many exact comparisons below $n_0$, this is an
all-dimensional certificate. For $A=K$, the tail test is only linear.
The compiler selects $B$ from the exact finite caps
\[
 B\le-\frac{n}{\gamma_n}-n^2-An
\]
and checks the tail polynomial after shifting by $n_0$. This is a proved
method of turning better seed certificates into better rational envelopes;
it is not an assumed Pad\'e approximation to an unknown extremal function.

\section{The proved limitation and what is not claimed}
Transporting an already transported seed does not amplify it:
\[
 K(k,h_k(K))=K.
\]
A new finite calculation must genuinely improve the seed, or add a new valid
constraint, to increase the transport constant. Adding a larger numerical
rank by itself is not a mathematical improvement.

There is also a stronger limitation theorem for the entire current recursive
spectral/mixed system: if $\widehat\gamma_n$ is its exact recursively optimized
floor, then
\[
 -\frac1n\le\widehat\gamma_n\le
 -\frac{n-1-32\log n}{n(n-1)+32\log n}
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
\]
Hence $n\widehat\gamma_n\to-1$. The exact proof and independent audits are in
the research package. This does not assert that the actual map minimum has
that order, or that the hierarchy converges to it. The technology above
delivers certified improvements to finite constants and rational correction
terms. No uniform $o(1/n)$ theorem is claimed.
