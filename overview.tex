\cataloguesection{Combinatorics}{1}

\resultentry{1}{McLeod's 1959 conjecture}{The paper proves McLeod's conjecture that $(h_{\ell+r}/h_\ell)^{1/r}$ is subadditive for all $\ell\ge0$ and $r\ge1$, where $h_k$ is the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree $k$. The first step shows, via a closed queueing network, that $h_k/h_{k-1}$ is convex; a tensorization argument then couples neighboring ratios tightly enough to make their geometric mean convex.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/mcleod-hk/v1/mcleod-hk-v1.pdf}{A Proof of McLeod's 1959 Conjecture on Complete Homogeneous Symmetric Ratios}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 5 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07302}{arXiv:2610.07302}}

\resultentry{2}{Monotone Tur\'an-ratios of Schur polynomials}{The paper proves that, unless all $n$ padded parts of $\lambda$ are equal, the Tur\'an-ratio $S_{\lambda,i}(x)^2/(S_{\lambda,i-1}(x)S_{\lambda,i+1}(x))$ is strictly increasing for $x>1$ and $1\le i<n$, where $S_{\lambda,i}(x)=s_\lambda(1^{[n-i]},x^{[i]})$ is the Schur polynomial in $n$ variables with $i$ arguments equal to $x$ and the rest equal to $1$. For one-row shapes this answers a question of Ait-Haddou on complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials; in that case a second proof, through the zeros of Meixner polynomials, gives an explicit positive formula for the logarithmic derivative of the ratio. It conjectures the analogue for Macdonald polynomials.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/sym-turan/v1/sym-turan-v1.pdf}{Monotonicity of Tur\'an-ratios of symmetric polynomials}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 7 October 2026}

\resultentry{3}{A Koteljanskii inequality for permanents}{The paper proves that $\operatorname{per}(A_{S\cup T})\operatorname{per}(A_{S\cap T})\ge\operatorname{per}(A_S)\operatorname{per}(A_T)$ for all $S,T\subseteq[n]$ when $A$ is an inverse $M$-matrix that becomes symmetric after a positive diagonal similarity; here $A_S$ is the principal submatrix indexed by $S$. The proof writes permanents as moments of a complex Gaussian vector and applies Ginibre's correlation inequality. An explicit counterexample shows that symmetry cannot be dropped.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/perm-m-matrix/v1/perm-m-matrix-v1.pdf}{A Koteljanskii inequality for permanents}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 2 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39979}{arXiv:2609.39979}}

\resultentry{4}{Majorization for normalized Jack and Macdonald polynomials}{The paper proves that if $\lambda$ majorizes $\mu$, then the Jack polynomial $P_\lambda$ in $n$ variables, normalized to equal one at $(1,\ldots,1)$, is at least the normalized $P_\mu$ on the positive orthant, for every $n$, and strictly so when the coordinates are distinct. For the conjectured Macdonald analogue, it proves the rank-two case, the case of a one-row partition against the hook below it for $t\le q$ in every rank, and, by exact certificates, the full statement in three variables for partitions of size at most eight. It shows that the Harish-Chandra--Itzykson--Zuber mechanism behind the Schur and Jack proofs has no direct finite-$q$ analogue. The full conjecture remains open.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/jackratio/v1/jackratio-v1.pdf}{On majorization inequalities for normalized Jack and Macdonald polynomials}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 8 October 2026}

\cataloguesection{Matrix analysis}{2}

\resultentry{5}{Lin's singular-value conjecture}{The paper proves Lin's conjecture that $\lambda_j((1-t)A+tB)\ge\sigma_j^{1/2}(A^{2(1-t)}B^{2t})$ for all positive semidefinite $A,B$, all $j$, and every $t\in[0,1]$. The case $t=\tfrac12$ is Drury's theorem, which answered a question of Bhatia and Kittaneh. The proof follows Drury's argument but replaces its block-matrix step, which works only at the midpoint, with an eigenvalue count based on an inertia identity and a one-variable inequality.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/linconj/v1/linconj-v1.pdf}{On Lin's Arithmetic--Geometric Means Conjecture for Singular Values}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 8 October 2026}

\resultentry{6}{Bellman's 1959 claim on Hua--Bellman matrices}{The paper shows that Bellman's 1959 strengthening of Hua's results on positive definiteness of the matrices $[\det(I-A_i^*A_j)^{-\alpha}]_{ij}$, for strict contractions $A_i$, rests on an erroneous proof and, by an explicit example, that it is false. It gives conditions under which $\det(I-A^*B)^{-\alpha}$ is a positive definite function: for complex contractions, the known sharp condition, with the integer exponents below Hua's range reached by an elementary argument; for real symmetric contractions, the part of Bellman's claim that survives. It also introduces a Poincar\'e-like distance on noncommuting strict contractions.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/hua/v1/hua-v1.pdf}{Positive definite functions of noncommuting contractions, Hua--Bellman matrices, and a new distance metric}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 2 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00056v2}{arXiv:2112.00056v2}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace Not AI-assisted}

\cataloguesection{Complex geometry}{3}

\resultentry{7}{Schur forms under decomposable curvature}{Griffiths asked in 1969 whether positive curvature of a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle forces positivity of its characteristic forms; Du's rank-nine example shows that a strictly Griffiths-positive metric can have a top Chern form that is negative on an open set. The paper proves that every Schur form is weakly nonnegative wherever the curvature is decomposable, that is, a sum of a completely positive map and a completely copositive map, with no splitting assumption. The algebraic input is the inequality $\Phi_n(H)\ge0$ for Finski's double mixed discriminant of every decomposable map $H$, obtained from a quaternionic Gaussian determinant formula. Beyond the decomposable cone, $\Phi_k\ge0$ on every square compression of $H$ is equivalent to $\Phi_n(H+S)\ge0$ for every entanglement-breaking map $S$; the class of maps satisfying this condition contains indecomposable maps but is not convex for $n\ge9$, since Du's map is an average of four of its members.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/qperm/v1/qperm-v1.pdf}{Weak positivity of Schur forms under decomposable curvature}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 8 October 2026}

\cataloguesection{Mathematical physics}{4}

\resultentry{8}{Petz's conjecture on the Kubo--Mori scalar curvature}{The paper proves Petz's 1994 conjecture that the scalar curvature of the Kubo--Mori metric on the invertible density matrices increases as the state becomes more mixed. Dittmann (2000) reduced the conjecture to the concavity of an explicit symmetric function $h$ of three positive variables, which he could support only by numerical tests; the paper proves that $h$ is strictly concave. The proof writes $h=-1/(8M)$ with $M$ positive and homogeneous of degree one, and shows that $1/M$ is a Stieltjes function along every line with positive base point and positive direction; Cauchy--Schwarz and homogeneity then give concavity, and the Stieltjes property reduces to an explicit inequality in two real variables, proved by elementary estimates for hyperbolic functions.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/kubo-metric/v1/kubo-metric-v1.pdf}{A Proof of Petz's Conjecture on the Scalar Curvature of the Kubo--Mori Metric}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 9 October 2026}

\resultentry{9}{The Gibilisco--Isola conjecture near the Kubo--Mori metric}{The paper proves the Gibilisco--Isola conjecture that the scalar curvature of the Wigner--Yanase--Dyson metric with parameter $p$ on the invertible density matrices increases under mixing for all $p$ in some interval $(1,1+\varepsilon)$, together with the mirror statement for $p\in(1-\varepsilon,1)$, with $\varepsilon>0$ independent of the dimension; the case $p\downarrow1$ is Petz's conjecture, result 8. For these $p$ the curvature is a concave function of the eigenvalues. By Dittmann's formula it suffices that one symmetric function $H_p$ of three positive variables is concave, which the paper proves by showing that $-1/H_p$ is a Stieltjes transform along every line in the positive cone, uniformly in $p$ near $1$; the uniformity near the boundary of the eigenvalue domain comes from an exact decomposition of $H_p$. The value of $\varepsilon$ is not explicit.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/wyd/v1/wyd-v1.pdf}{Wigner--Yanase--Dyson Metrics Have Monotone Curvature}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 9 October 2026}
