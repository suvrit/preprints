\resultentry{3}{A Koteljanskii inequality for permanents}{The paper proves that $\operatorname{per}(A_{S\cup T})\operatorname{per}(A_{S\cap T})\ge\operatorname{per}(A_S)\operatorname{per}(A_T)$ for all $S,T\subseteq[n]$ when $A$ is an inverse $M$-matrix that becomes symmetric after a positive diagonal similarity; here $A_S$ is the principal submatrix indexed by $S$. \resultentry{4}{Samorodnitsky's conjecture on permanents}{The paper proves the conjecture of Samorodnitsky, also stated by Gurvits and Samorodnitsky, that $\operatorname{per}\phi[A]\le1$ for every nonnegative matrix $A$ with unit row sums, where $\phi(x)=\Gamma(1+1/x)^{-x}$ is applied entrywise, with equality, up to permutations, exactly for direct sums of the scaled all-ones blocks $J_r/r$. When each row of $A$ is uniform on its support, this is the Br\'egman--Minc inequality. Equivalently, the permanent of a nonnegative matrix is at most the product of the Orlicz norms of its rows, for the norm that equals $(r!)^{1/r}$ on every zero-one row with $r$ ones; this refines Soules's extension of Br\'egman--Minc. Through H\"older's inequality and induction, the proof reduces to a scalar inequality on the simplex, proved with a positive integral representation of $x\log\Gamma(1+1/x)$. The paper also gives sharp bounds for rectangular permanents and matchings, and bounds the entropy of a random permutation by its row marginals.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/gurvits/v1/gurvits-v1.pdf}{A sharp Br\'egman--Minc bound for permanents}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 31 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2610.00731}{hexagon:2610.00731}}

\resultentry{5}{A monotone proof of van der Waerden's inequality}{The paper proves that the minimum total correlation of a random permutation with prescribed doubly stochastic marginal matrix $A$ never decreases when two rows or two columns of $A$ are averaged; this quantity is the sum of the row entropies of $A$ minus the largest entropy of such a permutation. Finitely many averages reach $J_n/n$, so the monotonicity gives a short proof of van der Waerden's inequality $\operatorname{per}A\ge n!/n^n$ with its equality case. The derivative along an average is nonnegative by the quadratic Alexandrov--Fenchel inequality for permanent cofactors, and nothing else specific to permanents is used; the same averaging therefore proves the Friedland--Tverberg inequalities and Gurvits's coefficient bound for Lorentzian polynomials, with lower bounds for mixed volumes and weighted counts of matroid bases. Each average keeps an exact nonnegative entropy remainder; for example, $\operatorname{per}A-n!/n^n\ge\tfrac12(n-2)!\,a^{n-2}\|A-J_n/n\|_F^2$, where $a$ is the smallest entry of $A$. A second averaging, of binary couplings, gives the stable-pairing bound of Anari and Oveis Gharan with an exact remainder, and for regular bipartite graphs improves Schrijver's bound by a factor exponential in the number of components.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/gurvits-lower/v1/gurvits-lower-v1.pdf}{A new proof of van der Waerden's inequality, and beyond}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 31 October 2026}

\resultentry{6}{Majorization for normalized Jack and Macdonald polynomials}{The paper proves that if $\lambda$ majorizes $\mu$, then the Jack polynomial $P_\lambda$ in $n$ variables, normalized to equal one at $(1,\ldots,1)$, is at least the normalized $P_\mu$ on the positive orthant, for every $n$, and strictly so when the coordinates are distinct. For the conjectured Macdonald analogue, it proves the rank-two case, the case of a one-row partition against the hook below it for $t\le q$ in every rank, and, by exact certificates, the full statement in three variables for partitions of size at most eight. It shows that the Harish-Chandra--Itzykson--Zuber mechanism behind the Schur and Jack proofs has no direct finite-$q$ analogue. The full conjecture remains open.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/jackratio/v1/jackratio-v1.pdf}{On majorization inequalities for normalized Jack and Macdonald polynomials}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 8 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2610.00725}{hexagon:2610.00725}}

\resultentry{7}{Positivity of the Macdonald $(q,t)$-permanent}{The paper introduces the Macdonald $(q,t)$-permanent $\operatorname{per}_{q,t}(A)=\sum_\sigma\prod_C\frac{1-t^{|C|}}{1-q^{|C|}}\prod_ia_{i\sigma(i)}$, the inner product over the cycles $C$ of $\sigma$, which contains the permanent and the Vere-Jones $\alpha$-permanent. A MacMahon-type master theorem gives its generating function, the product of determinant ratios $\prod_{r\ge0}\det(I-tq^rM)/\det(I-q^rM)$, and an expansion in irreducible immanants with the hook-content coefficients $\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(q^{i-1}-tq^{j-1})/(1-q^{h_{ij}})$. Their signs show that the kernel on $n$-tuples of vectors in $\mathbb C^d$ is positive semidefinite exactly for $t\in[0,q^{r-1}]\cup\{q,\ldots,q^{r-2}\}$, $r=\min(n,d)$, a $q$-analogue of the Wallach set. Nonnegativity on positive semidefinite matrices is subtler: on rank at most $r$, $\operatorname{per}_{q,q^\alpha}\ge0$ for every $q$ exactly when $\alpha$ is a Wallach exponent; at fixed $q$ the region is $t\le q$ in rank two and the kernel region in higher rank when $q$ exceeds an explicit threshold, while for small $q$ positivity reaches beyond the kernel region at every finite rank. Whether positivity in all ranks forces $t\in\{0,q,q^2,\ldots\}$ is open.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/macperm1/v1/macperm1-v1.pdf}{The Macdonald $(q,t)$-permanent I: Algebra and positivity}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 29 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2610.00729}{hexagon:2610.00729}}

\resultentry{8}{Probability laws of the Macdonald $(q,t)$-permanent}{The paper asks which probability laws built on the $\alpha$-permanent survive the Macdonald deformation of result 7, and where. The scalar gamma-type law survives on the whole square $0\le q,t<1$, through a Bernoulli--exponential series. The matrix law, a compound Wishart matrix with Laplace transform $\prod_{r\ge0}\det(I+tq^rS)/\det(I+q^rS)$, exists in every dimension exactly when $t\in\{0,q,q^2,\ldots\}$, where Gaussian columns with geometric weights construct it; elsewhere the moment of $(\det W)^2$ is negative in large dimension. In dimension $d$ the law requires $t\in[0,q^{d-1}]\cup\{q,\ldots,q^{d-2}\}$, the image of the Wallach set under $t=q^\alpha$, and in dimension two the region $[0,q]$ is exact; the paper conjectures sufficiency in higher dimension as a $q$-analogue of Gindikin's theorem. Discrete laws survive on larger regions: occupation laws of a length-weighted loop soup throughout the square, coefficient laws of rank-$d$ kernels on that region with infinite divisibility independent of $(q,t)$, Cox processes with correlation functions $\operatorname{per}_{q,t}$, and a determinantal Schur measure on partitions with at most $d$ rows.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/macperm2/v1/macperm2-v1.pdf}{The Macdonald $(q,t)$-permanent II: Probability laws and their regions of validity}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 29 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2610.00730}{hexagon:2610.00730}}

\resultentry{9}{A fusion inequality for Macdonald plethysms}{The paper proves that the plethysm $H_m[\widetilde H_\mu]$ of a modified Macdonald polynomial by the modified Hall--Littlewood polynomial $H_m$ lies above the Macdonald polynomial $\widetilde H_{m\mu}$ obtained by fusing $m$ copies of $\widetilde H_\mu$: the quotient $(H_m[\widetilde H_\mu]-\widetilde H_{m\mu})/(1-q^m)$ is Schur positive with coefficients in $\mathbb N[q,t]$ and is the character of an explicit module, the fusion defect. A symmetry of the modified Macdonald polynomials reverses this comparison at the cost of a degree shift, whose least value the paper determines. For one-row shapes the two comparisons give a shifted form of Bergeron's $q$-analog of Foulkes' conjecture: $(H_b[H_a]-q^{a(a-1)\lfloor b/2\rfloor}H_a[H_b])/(1-q)$ is Schur positive for all $a,b\ge2$, where the conjecture asks for this without the factor $q^{a(a-1)\lfloor b/2\rfloor}$ when $a\le b$. A shift linear in $b$ also holds for Bergeron's rectangular version.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/qfoulkes1/v1/qfoulkes1-v1.pdf}{The $q$-Foulkes problem I: A fusion inequality for Macdonald plethysms}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 26 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2610.00722}{hexagon:2610.00722}}

\resultentry{10}{Schur positivity with high probability for the $q$-Foulkes conjecture}{Bergeron's $q$-analog of Foulkes' conjecture asserts that $(H_b[H_a]-H_a[H_b])/(1-q)$ is Schur positive for $a\le b$, where $H_n$ is the modified Hall--Littlewood polynomial. The paper views the conjecture probabilistically: on each irreducible representation of $S_{ab}$ the two plethysms induce degree distributions, and positivity means that the first is stochastically smaller than the second. It proves that, for a Plancherel-random irreducible representation and a degree sampled from its graded multiplicity, the corresponding coefficient is negative with probability at most $C\exp(-c(ab)^{1/3})$, uniformly in $a\le b$; consequently the conjecture holds after deleting a fraction at most $C\exp(-c(ab)^{1/3})$ of each of the two graded modules. Exact conditional means and variances give complete positivity for explicit width and height families.}{\href{https://github.com/suvrit/preprints/blob/main/preprints/qfoulkes2/v1/qfoulkes2-v1.pdf}{The $q$-Foulkes problem II: Schur positivity with high probability}\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace v1, 28 October 2026\enspace\textperiodcentered\enspace\href{https://hexagonmath.org/abs/2610.00723}{hexagon:2610.00723}}

\resultentry{11}{Foulkes' conjecture with a graded inner factor}{The paper proves that surjectivity of the canonical map $\operatorname{Sym}^b(\operatorname{Sym}^aV)\to\operatorname{Sym}^a(\operatorname{Sym}^bV)$, which gives Foulkes' conjecture that $h_b[h_a]-h_a[h_b]$ is Schur positive for $a\le b$, survives grading of the inner factor: whenever the map is surjective, $h_b[H_a]-H_a[h_b]$ is Schur positive with coefficients in $\mathbb N[q]$, where $H_a$ is the modified Hall--Littlewood polynomial, and the same holds for modified Macdonald polynomials. \resultentry{14}{The $p$-Bures--Wasserstein distance and the Lin--van den Driessche conjecture}{The paper proves that $D_p(A,B)=(\operatorname{tr}A^p+\operatorname{tr}B^p-2\|A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\|_p^p)^{1/2}$, which is the Bures--Wasserstein distance at $p=1$ and the Hilbert--Schmidt distance at $p=2$, is a metric on the positive semidefinite matrices for every $1\le p\le2$. \resultentry{15}{Tsallis metrics on density matrices}{The paper proves that $d_\alpha(\rho,\sigma)=|2-\|\rho^\alpha+\sigma^\alpha\|_{1/\alpha}|^{1/2}$ is a metric on density matrices for $1/2\le\alpha\le2$, $\alpha\ne1$; up to the factor $|1-\alpha|$, $d_\alpha^2$ is the least total Tsallis relative entropy of order $\alpha$ from two states to a common state, and $\alpha\uparrow1$ recovers the quantum Jensen--Shannon metric. \resultentry{16}{A spectral Blakley--Roy inequality for nonnegative matrices}{The paper proves that $\rho(AB)^k\le\rho(A^kB^k)$ for all real symmetric matrices $A,B$ with nonnegative entries and every integer $k\ge1$, where $\rho$ is the spectral radius; for $B=uu^T$ this is the Blakley--Roy inequality, and the general case was conjectured by Pourya Habibzadeh. \resultentry{17}{Schur forms under decomposable curvature}{Griffiths asked in 1969 whether positive curvature of a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle forces positivity of its characteristic forms; Du's rank-nine example shows that a strictly Griffiths-positive metric can have a top Chern form that is negative on an open set. The paper proves that every Schur form is weakly nonnegative wherever the curvature is decomposable, that is, a sum of a completely positive map and a completely copositive map, with no splitting assumption.
