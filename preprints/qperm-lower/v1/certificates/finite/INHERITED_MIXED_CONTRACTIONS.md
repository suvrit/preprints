# Rank-six and rank-seven quantum-permanent bounds from mixed contractions
## A signed quantum-occupancy interpretation and exact recursive certificates

**Research supplement — 9 October 2026.**

## 1. Scope and certified conclusions

For a complex-linear ordinary positive bistochastic map `H:M_n(C)->M_n(C)`, write

\[
\Gamma_n(H)=\det(\partial_X)\det H(X),\qquad
\Phi_n(H)=\Gamma_n(H)/n!,\qquad
\mu_n=\min_H\Phi_n(H).
\]

The variables in `partial_X` are independent complex matrix entries. Positivity is not complete positivity. This note proves

\[
\boxed{\Phi_6(H)\ge
-\frac{129358525188828749}{2325438043571344896}
=-0.055627594786470176\ldots,}
\tag{1.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{\Phi_7(H)\ge
-\frac{5408353173620987686795318309001825}
{97775984492669811845528183241796197}
=-0.05531371738861343\ldots.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

These improve the preceding projection-spectral bounds
`-5585861/72987571` and `-1337749/16473675` by about 27.3% and 31.9% of the permitted negative magnitude. They are exact optima of the recursive linear relaxations defined below, **not** established values of `mu_6` or `mu_7`.

No proof of unrestricted nonnegativity in either dimension and no negatively signed map in dimension six or seven is asserted. Zero is attained in both dimensions by the pinched direct sum of

\[
R_3(X)=(\operatorname{tr}(X)I_3-X^{\mathsf T})/2
\]

and the identity map on the remaining coordinates. Its odd skew Kraus pencil has identically zero determinant. Thus the current conclusions are `gamma_6 <= mu_6 <= 0` and `gamma_7 <= mu_7 <= 0`.

The new input is a way to transfer a signed lower bound in dimension `k` to **all coefficients of mixed determinant contractions in larger dimensions**. Wan's unrestricted rank-three theorem supplies the initial zero bound [W]. The finite model then propagates newly certified bounds in dimensions four and five into dimensions six and seven.

The occupancy interpretation below is exact, but its signed statistic is not a coupon-collection probability. Explicit balanced classical occupancy laws have negative values of that statistic and are feasible in our retained relaxation. They are not negative quantum-permanent maps.

## 2. The inherited orbital and projection-spectral relaxation

Average the determinant polynomial, not the map:

\[
f_H(X)=\int_{U(n)}\det H(UXU^*)\,dU.
\]

On diagonal inputs write

\[
f_H(x)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}w_\lambda q_\lambda(x),\qquad
q_\lambda=m_\lambda/m_\lambda(1^n).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The matrices `H(u_i u_i*)` are PSD, trace one, and sum to the identity. Rank-one decomposition and Cauchy--Binet give nonnegative monomial coefficients, whose sum is one. Consequently

\[
w_\lambda\ge0,\qquad \sum_\lambda w_\lambda=1.
\]

The determinant differential extracts the column Schur coefficient. With

\[
r_\lambda=\frac{(-1)^{n-\ell(\lambda)}}{\binom n{\ell(\lambda)}},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{\Phi_n(H)=\sum_\lambda r_\lambda w_\lambda.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For example, the Frobenius formula gives
`[s_(1^n)]m_lambda = (-1)^(n-ell) ell!/prod_j m_j(lambda)!`, whereas
`m_lambda(1^n)=n!/((n-ell)! prod_j m_j(lambda)!)`; their quotient is `r_lambda`.

Gurvits's mixed-discriminant lower theorem [G] yields

\[
w_{(1^n)}\ge n!/n^n.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a rank-`k` projection, ordinary positivity gives

\[
0\preceq H(P)\preceq I,\qquad \operatorname{tr}H(P)=k.
\]

The convex hull of the characteristic coefficient vectors of these spectra is generated by

\[
(1^{[k]},0^{[n-k]})
\]

and

\[
v_{a,b}=\left(1^{[a]},((k-a)/b)^{[b]},0^{[n-a-b]}\right),
\quad 0\le a<k,\quad k-a<b\le n-a.
\tag{2.4}
\]

There are `1+k(n-k)` generators. To prove the hull assertion, minimize a symmetric multiaffine function of a spectrum with fixed trace, choosing a minimizer of maximal squared norm. Two distinct interior coordinates enter as `A+B(u+v)+Cuv`. At a constrained stationary point they force `C=0`, allowing a move to the boundary that increases squared norm. Thus every interior coordinate has the same value. Applying the same argument to the negative functional proves equality of convex hulls.

For `lambda` padded with zeros, define

\[
B_{k,j,\lambda}=\binom nk^{-1}
\sum_{|I|=k}\binom{\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i}{j}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Then `B w` is the expected characteristic coefficient vector of `H(UP_kU*)`. Indeed, it is the coefficient of `t^(n-j)` in `f_H(t 1+1_[k])`. Hence the original relaxation imposes

\[
\sum_\lambda B_{k,j,\lambda}w_\lambda
=\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{n,k}}e_j(v)\pi_{k,v},\qquad
\pi_{k,v}\ge0,\quad\sum_v\pi_{k,v}=1,
\tag{2.6}
\]

for `1<=k<=floor(n/2)` and `2<=j<=n`. The constraints for `j=0,1` follow from normalization and trace. All coefficients in (2.5)--(2.6) are rational. This model is inherited from *Optimizing the signed quantum-permanent lower bound through projection spectra*; it is reproduced here to make the strengthened certificate self-contained.

## 3. An exact quantum coupon model

Fix an input frame `U`, and set `rho_i=H(u_i u_i*)`. This is a POVM:
`rho_i>=0`, `sum rho_i=I`, and `tr rho_i=1`.

On `(C^n)^(tensor n)`, let

\[
|\mathrm{Alt}_n\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt{n!}}
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)
 e_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\sigma(n)}.
\]

Measuring the POVM on its `n` tensor factors gives labels `I_1,...,I_n`, with probabilities

\[
\Pr(I_1=i_1,\ldots,I_n=i_n\mid U)
=\langle\mathrm{Alt}_n|
 \rho_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{i_n}
 |\mathrm{Alt}_n\rangle.
\]

They are exchangeable, with uniform one-label marginals, but generally are not independent. If `N_i` is the occupancy count, then

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\left[\prod_i x_i^{N_i}\mid U\right]
=\det\left(\sum_i x_i\rho_i\right).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This follows from `A^(tensor n)` acting as `det A` on the one-dimensional top exterior power. It also follows directly by decomposing each effect into rank-one vectors and applying Cauchy--Binet: the microscopic selected vectors have projection-determinantal weights, and `i` records their color.

After averaging `U`, the distribution of the sorted occupancy counts is exactly `w_lambda` in (2.1). If `L` is the number of distinct labels, (2.2) becomes

\[
\boxed{\Phi_n(H)=\mathbb E\frac{(-1)^{n-L}}{\binom nL}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The expectation is over the input frame and this correlated measurement. In particular,
`Pr(L=n)=w_(1^n)>=n!/n^n`. Covering all labels contributes positively, but the other occupancy levels have alternating signs. A coverage-probability lower bound alone is therefore not a nonnegativity proof.

## 4. Transferring nonpositive lower bounds to unnormalized maps

### Lemma 4.1

Suppose `gamma_k<=0` and `Phi_k(L)>=gamma_k` for every positive bistochastic `L:M_k->M_k`. Then every ordinary positive `K:M_k->M_k` satisfies

\[
\boxed{\Phi_k(K)\ge\gamma_k\det K(I_k).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** First make `K` strictly positivity improving. There is a `c>0` with
`K(X)>=c tr(X)I` for PSD `X`. The capacity objective `det K(X)` on
`X>0, det X=1` has a minimizer `X_0`: its sublevel sets have bounded trace and hence eigenvalues bounded away from zero. Let `C=K(X_0)`.

The first-order equation of `log det K(X)` under `log det X=0` is

\[
K^*(C^{-1})=X_0^{-1}.
\]

The Lagrange multiplier equals one on pairing with `X_0`. Thus

\[
L(Z)=C^{-1/2}K(X_0^{1/2}ZX_0^{1/2})C^{-1/2}
\]

is positive and bistochastic, and determinant covariance gives

\[
\Phi_k(K)=\det C\,\Phi_k(L)
\ge\gamma_k\det C
\ge\gamma_k\det K(I).
\]

The last inequality uses `gamma_k<=0` and the minimizing property of `C`. For general `K`, apply this to `K+epsilon tr(.)I` and let `epsilon` decrease to zero. QED.

The sign restriction on `gamma_k` is essential. One may use `gamma_2=gamma_3=0`, but must not substitute the positive bistochastic floor `1/4` in dimension two into (4.1). The zero bound for arbitrary positive maps on `M_3` is Wan's theorem [W]; dimension two follows also by pinched extension to dimension three.

The argument is the capacity/Sinkhorn normalization mechanism from positive-map scaling, written out here to avoid assuming that compatible compressions themselves are bistochastic. Notice that the resulting negative bounds also apply to merely unital positive maps, since then `det K(I)=1`.

## 5. A mixed-differential theorem

Fix `1<=k<n` and the coordinate embedding `V:C^k->C^n`, with `P=VV*`. For an ordinary positive `H`, set

\[
A(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_iH(E_{ii}),\qquad
F(x,Z)=\det\bigl(A(x)+H(VZV^*)\bigr).
\]

### Theorem 5.1

Under the assumption of Lemma 4.1, every monomial coefficient in

\[
\boxed{
\left.\det(\partial_Z)F(x,Z)\right|_{Z=0}
-\gamma_k\left.\partial_s^k F(x,sI_k)\right|_{s=0}
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

is nonnegative.

**Proof.** Write each PSD `H(E_ii)` as a sum of rank-one matrices. Expand the determinant by the baseline rank-one terms. Its part homogeneous of degree `k` in `Z` is a sum indexed by choices of `n-k` independent baseline output vectors:

\[
\sum_S x^{\nu(S)}\det(A_S^*A_S)
\det\bigl(U_S^*H(VZV^*)U_S\bigr).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Here `U_S` is an isometry onto the orthogonal complement of their span. A dependent choice has zero Gram determinant. Every weight is nonnegative, and each
`K_S(Z)=U_S^*H(VZV*)U_S` is an ordinary positive map on `M_k`.

The first derivative in (5.1) contributes `Gamma_k(K_S)`; the second contributes `k! det K_S(I)`. Lemma 4.1 makes their difference nonnegative for each summand individually. Terms of different `Z`-degree vanish on differentiation and evaluation at zero. Grouping the nonnegative contributions by `x^{nu(S)}` proves the coefficientwise assertion. QED.

This is a common-map statement: no tensor positivity of `H`, no independent replacement of its coefficients, and no complete positivity of `H` are used.

## 6. From matrix derivatives to finite orbital inequalities

Define the active-variable Vandermonde and two operators by

\[
\Delta_k(x)=\prod_{1\le i<j\le k}(x_i-x_j),\qquad
\mathscr D_k f=\Delta_k^{-1}
\partial_{x_1}\cdots\partial_{x_k}(\Delta_k f),
\]

\[
\mathscr T_k f=(\partial_{x_1}+\cdots+\partial_{x_k})^k f.
\tag{6.1}
\]

On polynomials symmetric in the first `k` variables, the quotient defining `D_k` is polynomial: the differentiated numerator is alternating. The radial determinant identity says that `D_k` is the restriction to diagonal matrices of the determinant differential in the active `k`-by-`k` matrix block.

Here is one normalization proof. In dimension `k`, Schur polynomials are Fischer orthogonal, with

\[
\langle s_\lambda,s_\mu\rangle_F
=\delta_{\lambda\mu}\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(k+j-i).
\]

This follows by expanding the Frobenius character formula and using the Gaussian monomial pairing. Multiplication by `det` is adjoint to `det(partial)`, and `det s_mu=s_(mu+(1^k))`. Consequently

\[
\det(\partial)s_\lambda
=\left(\prod_{i=1}^k(\lambda_i+k-i)\right)s_{\lambda-(1^k)},
\]

with the answer zero when `lambda_k=0`. The same formula for `D_k` follows immediately by differentiating each row of the alternant `Delta_k s_lambda`. This proves the radial identity on a basis, including coefficients depending on the inactive variables.

For the finite certificates here there is also a fully independent algebraic check of **every** radial coefficient, by differentiating power-sum trace monomials in matrix units. It does not use the radial identity as an input; see Section 10.

Averaging Theorem 5.1 over input frames gives

\[
\boxed{
(\mathscr D_k-\gamma_k\mathscr T_k)f_H(x)
\text{ has nonnegative monomial coefficients.}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is a collection of linear inequalities in the orbital weights `w_lambda`. The degree is `n-k`; by separate symmetry, a coefficient is indexed by a pair of partitions `(alpha,nu)` describing the active and inactive exponents. Its exact row is

\[
R_{k,\alpha,\nu;\lambda}
=[x^{\alpha}y^{\nu}]
(\mathscr D_k-\gamma_k\mathscr T_k)q_\lambda(x,y).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The program reconstructs these rows by the expansion

\[
m_\lambda(x,y)=\sum_{\mu\uplus\nu=\lambda}m_\mu(x)m_\nu(y)
\]

and exact integer Vandermonde division. No factorial or multiplicity factor is inserted in this multiset-split identity.

### One readable three-coupon inequality

Using `gamma_3=0`, the coefficient of `x_4...x_n` in `D_3 f_H` gives

\[
\boxed{
 n(n-1)(n-2)w_{(1^n)}
 -2(n-2)w_{(2,1^{n-2})}
 +2w_{(3,1^{n-3})}\ge0.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This compares complete coverage, one double occupation, and one triple occupation. It is not implied by the previously retained complete-coverage floor. In fact, the exact old optimal profiles violate the left-hand side by

\[
-\frac{231018480}{72987571}\quad(n=6),\qquad
-\frac{14796488}{3294735}\quad(n=7).
\]

Thus the mixed rank-three information removes the previous optima for a concrete reason. The full certificate uses all coefficient inequalities (6.2), not only (6.4).

## 7. The recursive linear program and exact bounds

Start with `gamma_2=gamma_3=0`. For `n=4,5,6,7`, minimize (2.2) subject to the orbital simplex, the mixed-discriminant floor (2.3), the projection-spectral constraints (2.6), and every coefficient inequality (6.2) for `2<=k<n`, using the previously certified `gamma_k`.

Rows all of whose coefficients are nonnegative are omitted, since they follow from `w>=0`. Each retained inequality is normalized by a positive rational factor and gets one nonnegative slack. The model is thus `min c^T x` subject to `Ax=b, x>=0` with rational data.

The exact outcomes are:

| n | equality rows | nonnegative variables | exact recursive bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 17 | 22 | `-3/62` |
| 5 | 28 | 36 | `-77957/1378367` |
| 6 | 54 | 71 | `-129358525188828749/2325438043571344896` |
| 7 | 87 | 111 | `-5408353173620987686795318309001825/97775984492669811845528183241796197` |

The stored rational primal and dual vectors satisfy

\[
x_*\ge0,\quad Ax_*=b,\quad A^{\mathsf T}y_*\le c,\quad
c^{\mathsf T}x_*=b^{\mathsf T}y_*.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore each common value is the exact LP optimum. Since the orbital data of every valid map are feasible, weak duality gives a universal map bound. Inducting on `n` validates the use of each new value in later models.

For the requested dimensions, the certified interval is

\[
\gamma_6\le\mu_6\le0,\qquad
\gamma_7\le\mu_7\le0.
\]

No feasibility assertion about the optimal LP weights as an actual map is made. Their negative values establish a limitation of this specified relaxation, not of the sign conjecture itself.

## 8. Why generic coupon laws still cannot prove the target sign

Take the commuting trace-one PSD effects determined by the doubly stochastic cyclic matrix

\[
C=\frac{I+P_n}{2},\qquad
\rho_i=\operatorname{diag}(C_{1i},\ldots,C_{ni}).
\]

Their determinant polynomial is

\[
p_{\rm cyc}(x)=2^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^n(x_i+x_{i+1}),
\quad x_{n+1}=x_1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

One may realize the counts by `n` independent sources, source `i` choosing label `i` or `i+1` equally. Randomly ordering the sources gives exchangeable labels. This is not iid uniform coupon sampling.

Encode the choices by a cyclic binary string. If it has `2j` changes, it creates `j` empty labels and `j` double occupancies, so `L=n-j`. There are `2 binom(n,2j)` such strings. Thus

\[
\Pr(L=n-j)=\frac{\binom n{2j}}{2^{n-1}},\quad
0\le j\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The signed scores are

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\frac{(-1)^{6-L}}{\binom6L}=-\frac{11}{640},
\qquad
\mathbb E\frac{(-1)^{7-L}}{\binom7L}=-\frac1{120}.}
\tag{8.3}
\]

These are legitimate balanced, commuting determinant occupancy laws; the generating polynomial (8.1) is even real stable as a product of positive linear forms. Full coupon coverage has probability `2^(1-n)>=n!/n^n` in dimensions six and seven. Neither fact forces the signed score to be nonnegative.

**They are not counterexamples for positive maps.** For the actual EB map

\[
K_C(X)=\operatorname{diag}(C\operatorname{diag}X),
\]

one has

\[
\Phi_n(K_C)=\frac{\operatorname{per}C}{n!}
=\frac{2^{1-n}}{n!}>0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

The one-frame polynomial (8.1), even after averaging coordinate permutations, is not the full unitary orbital polynomial of `K_C`. Equations (8.3) and (8.4) evaluate different objects. The count profiles in (8.3) are not asserted to be realizable as full orbital profiles of some other positive map.

For an explicit limitation of the present method, the verifier also checks that the permutation-symmetrized profiles from (8.1) are feasible in **all** the recursive constraints in Section 7. Their projection-spectral weights are obtained exactly by averaging the spectra of `C 1_S`, which have only values `0,1/2,1` and belong to (2.4). The mixed-coefficient inequalities are checked rationally. Thus the retained data themselves permit a negative signed score even in these concrete occupancy profiles.

The missing information is how one linear positive map generates compatible output matrices when the input basis is continuously changed, not just the validity of separate occupancy laws or spectral marginals. The current constraints capture some of that information, through mixed contractions, but not enough to establish `Phi_6>=0` or `Phi_7>=0`.

## 9. Interpretation and attribution

The result is a universal lower-bound improvement rather than another special family of positive maps. The key addition is the mixed-differential transfer: known low-dimensional sign theorems and negative lower bounds impose inequalities on larger-dimensional coefficient profiles. Exact rational duality then turns those inequalities into the constants (1.1)--(1.2).

The quantum-permanent invariant, its Fischer interpretation, and capacity-based normalization are established inputs in Gurvits's framework [QP]. The mixed-discriminant lower bound is Gurvits's theorem [G]. Wan's unrestricted rank-three result [W] is the substantive starting sign theorem. Determinant alternation, radial differential identities, and elementary LP duality are standard algebraic tools; none is presented as newly invented.

The measurement construction in Section 3 is a top-exterior-power/determinantal occupancy model. It is not identified with the sample-complexity problem of learning an unknown set from quantum samples studied under the title *Quantum Coupon Collector*. Its purpose here is to give an exact probabilistic reading of our coefficient weights and of the sign obstruction.

No new arbitrary-rank nonnegativity theorem, no smallest-rank counterexample claim, and no claim of a globally optimal map is made. In particular the sign questions in ranks six and seven remain unresolved by this supplement.

q_\lambda=\frac1{m_\lambda(1^n)\prod_jm_j(\lambda)!}
