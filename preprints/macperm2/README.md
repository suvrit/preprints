> **Abstract.** The Macdonald $`(q,t)`$-permanent replaces the constant cycle weight of the Vere-Jones $`\alpha`$-permanent by $`(1-t^k)/(1-q^k)`$ for a cycle of length $`k`$. We determine which of the probability laws built on the $`\alpha`$-permanent survive this deformation, and where. The scalar gamma-type law survives on the whole parameter square, but the matrix law, a compound Wishart matrix, is more fragile. We prove that it exists in every dimension exactly when $`t\in\{0,q,q^2,\ldots\}`$, and in dimension two exactly when $`t\le q`$. We conjecture that in each fixed dimension the matrix law exists exactly on the region that we show to be necessary, a $`q`$-analogue of Gindikin's theorem.
